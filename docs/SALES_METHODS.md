# The sales methods behind the deal record

Why the deal fields, checklists and reasons are the ones they are. Only methods whose content could be checked are used,
each is named with its source, and where sources disagree that is said. Nothing here is a claim about results.

## Qualification: the 12 answers on every deal

| On the deal | Comes from | What it asks |
|---|---|---|
| Problem today | Gap selling (current state); MEDDPICC "Identify pain"; BANT "Need" | What is broken or costing them now, and why |
| What they want instead | Gap selling (future state) | The result they describe in their own words |
| What it is worth to them | MEDDPICC "Metrics" | The numbers they gave: money, hours, leads, errors |
| Budget | BANT "Budget" | What they said they can spend |
| Timeline | BANT "Timeline" | When they want it live |
| Why now | (a compelling event, a common sales term) | The deadline or event that means it cannot wait |
| Who signs | MEDDPICC "Economic buyer"; BANT "Authority" | The person who approves the money |
| Champion | MEDDPICC "Champion" | Who wants this and pushes for you inside |
| How they will choose | MEDDPICC "Decision criteria" | What they compare on |
| Steps to a yes | MEDDPICC "Decision process" | Who sees it, in what order, by when |
| Paperwork | MEDDPICC "Paper process" | PO, vendor registration, legal review, contract |
| Competitors | MEDDPICC "Competition" | Who else, including doing nothing or doing it themselves |

- **BANT** (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline) is the qualification checklist associated with IBM.
- **MEDDIC** came out of PTC (Parametric Technology Corporation) in the mid-1990s; most sources credit Dick Dunkel, with
  Jack Napoli, under John McMahon. The extra P (Paper process) and C (Competition) of **MEDDPICC** were added later; the
  sources found do not date those additions. Much of what is written about MEDDIC comes from firms that sell training in
  it, so their claims about results are not repeated here.
- **Gap selling** is from Keenan, *Gap Selling: Getting the Customer to Yes* (2018): understand the buyer's current state
  and the future state they want; the gap between them is the value. The future state should come from the buyer.

## Why deals are lost

The default lost reasons start from Zig Ziglar's five obstacles to a sale, usually traced to *Zig Ziglar's Secrets of
Closing the Sale* (1984): **no need, no money, no hurry, no desire, no trust**. They are followed by the ways deals are
lost in practice: price, a competitor, doing it themselves, no decision at all, going silent, a scope that does not fit,
losing your contact, backing out after a yes. The list is yours to edit (Sales settings > Reasons); keeping it a fixed
list is what makes the reasons countable in Insights.

## The closing record

The Won checklist asks for what every services business needs in writing before work starts: who you work with, the final
amount, start and delivery, the exact scope (what is included and what is not), payment terms, and the contract or PO.
"Why they chose you" and "what happened" on a loss are kept because win/loss patterns only show when the reasons are
recorded at the moment of closing.

## A method referenced but not built in as fields

- **Alex Hormozi's C.L.O.S.E.R.** framework for sales calls is usually given as: Clarify why they are there, Label their
  problem, Overview their past pain (what they tried), Sell the vacation (the outcome, not the process), Explain away
  concerns, Reinforce the decision. Secondary sources differ on the wording and on the final R, and most are summaries
  rather than his own material. It describes how to run a call, not what to record, so it is not a set of fields here;
  the activity log's "What was discussed" is where its answers go.
